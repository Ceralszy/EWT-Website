1. Taming the Flexbox "Stretch"
The Trap: By default, Flexbox wants all its children to stretch to the exact height of the parent container. This is why your hover highlights were turning into giant, floor-to-ceiling blocks.

The Fix: Using align-items: center; on the parent (.nav-bar). This explicitly tells the children to stop stretching, keep their natural size, and float in the middle.

2. The inline-flex Shrink-Wrap
The Trap: Standard display: flex acts like a block element, taking up wider lanes of space and fighting for alignment.

The Fix: Changing the .menu-container to display: inline-flex;. This forced the box to perfectly "shrink-wrap" around the hamburger lines and the "Menu" text, making your hover state look like a crisp, snug button.

3. The Hidden <p> Tag Margins
The Trap: Browsers sneakily apply default margins to paragraph tags. That invisible space was blowing up your flexbox math and making your buttons way too tall.

The Fix: Swapping to inline elements (like <span> or just raw text inside a <div>), or explicitly setting margin: 0 and line-height: 1 to strip away the browser's hidden styles.

4. The space-between Butterfly Effect
The Trap: When using justify-content: space-between, adding margin or padding to one side doesn't just move that one item—it forces Flexbox to recalculate the entire equation, shifting the middle item off-center.

The Fix: Understanding that space-between is an active math equation. If you need a perfectly locked center, you use auto-margins (margin: 0 auto) instead of space-between.

6. The "Ghost" of Absolute PositioningThe Trap: Giving an element position: absolute completely removes it from the physical layout. The parent container treats it like it has $0$ width and $0$ height, which breaks hover states and container sizes.The Fix: (As noted in your comment!) Realizing that absolute ignores all flex rules, and sticking to pure Flexbox for structural alignment whenever possible.

7. Uneven PNG sizes, the social media logos all have different sizes. How do we make it so that all of them are of equal sizing? You could do it the designer way that is fixing the source files.

Or through CSS  by using a mixture of these codes:

background-image: url("") -- This is the image you want
background-size: x% -- adjust this accordingly, depending on how you want, or if you want all the symbols to look even.
background-repeat: no-repeat -- by default, tinkering with background size causes unwanted tiling. By having this, it stops that.
background-position: center -- after removing the tiling, you'd notice how it is off center despite the align-self being :center and justify-self:center. use this to make sure its at the 				middle of the current rectangle.

10. Every web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari) automatically adds an invisible 8px margin around the entire <body> tag by default. Browsers do this so that if someone makes a plain text website without any CSS, the words don't smash right against the glass edge of your monitor.
